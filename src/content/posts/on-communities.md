# On communities

Coming from a small village I grew up taking the community around me for granted 
(and sometimes even being burdened by it 🥶). 
The village is and was always there: sometimes as a peaceful home to come back to, 
sometimes as a golden cage I desperately wanted to escape - especially as 
a teenager who struggled to fit in and make friends. 
I indeed *escaped* when I first moved to go to University: 
I was ambitious,
curious to try and live in a real city,
eager to become and engineer and find friends that liked maths, arts, and philosophy. 
Growing up, I never felt like these dimensions could find a space in such a small community: 
I was unable to find role models, was fascinated by cultures far from my own, 
and ultimately decided that *yeeting* without looking back was the only sensible choice. 
*Aaah, the things one does at 18* :)

It's been almost 10 years since then, I've lived in a bunch of different places and ended up looking back. A lot. 
I owe a lot to that scared and ambitious 18-year-old girl - starting with the audacity to take what 
felt like a step into the unknown - 
but I find myself wishing she had taken better care of what was around her.
With enough distance and perspective (and therapy) I realized how I was *missing* that community, 
wondering where I belong, what belonging even means, but most of all: how does one build a community?

## Belonging 

When I first moved to the UK, I was talking to some friends about the feeling of stuck-ness
I had growing up in a small village: going back home I used to feel uncomfortable, thinking of myself like an outcast, feeling ashamed of my life choices - which definitely did not help with building and maintaining whatever relations I had with my peers there. 

And then a [friend](https://www.lopoukhine.com/) said to me: 
> Even if you feel like you don't fit in, you are still part of the village, 
> and the village also includes you 

I didn't really understand what he meant. 

But I kept thinking about this sentence a lot, and slowly I started to accept the discomfort, shame, and contradictions that I felt whenever I went back. 
As my dad puts it: *you should only be ashamed when you're hurting someone*.

So the time came for the a tradition according to which people born in the same year gather once a year and celebrate ("coscrizione"). There are not that many of us, and it's basically our primary school class. For the longest time I used to not participate in this gathering, fearing I would feel like an outcast and like I did not belong. But then it occurred to me: I am from that village as much as everyone else, and there my roots are - and will always be!
**So, after almost 10 years since moving away from the valley, I went. And what an amazing evening it was!**

Turns out, accepting the discomfort, accepting that those are my roots no-matter-what was enough to enjoy a night out with people I hadn't seen in a long time. And while one does not rekindle these relations overnight, it this was an important step for me, and a way to remind me that, in my own way, I belong there. 
## Building communities

And so, in my silly little life abroad, I like to put effort in building a community. 
I came to the (cliche) conclusion that communities are born when individuals sacrifice at least some of their freedom in favour of others. And this is pretty painful, especially when we have our busy lives to live and issues to deal with!
However, I found that making time to, say, invite a bunch of friends over even when I would like to sit alone in silence, came with infinite rewards of insightful conversations, ridiculous conversations, and sometimes watching some silly episodes of a silly series together. 
And at the same time, having people over when the house is not perfect, when we haven't had time to clean the floors... was hard for the control-freak side of me, but what a liberation! To have people over freely, with less worries, and focusing on everyone having a good time. 

> The village is (also) what we make of it

The community we build around us is also what we make of it, and we have a lot of power to improve it, evolve it, and make it **our own** - for good and for worse. 
Without drifting toward a God complex, we have responsibility to *create around us* the communities we would like to *see around us*, and this year I finally realised how powerful this is. 
Slowly, as we put more effort into curating our community, we saw people coming over more frequently, friends from different groups mingling and merging, and ultimately we got this powerful feeling that, if we need a hand, we'll find one. That we are not alone. 

So when I think about my neighbours in the valley, my primary school friends, my family's friends, I remember I belong there: making that **community** is also my responsibility.
